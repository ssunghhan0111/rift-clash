The Hero Bay, and a keep whose SHAPE means something

Two features, and one bug found by each of them.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
1 · MASTERY NOW UNLOCKS SOMETHING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The start screen has been saying "Mastery 12 · next unlock at 14" for months.
There was nothing at 14. `RC.MASTERY` gated nothing at all — config.js said so
out loud in a comment — so the game was making a promise every single session
and quietly not keeping it, which is worse than never having made it.

It keeps it now. Each hero has alternate Q and E skills and a wider set of
ultimate upgrades, unlocked by Mastery and chosen in a Loadout drawer beside
the Wardrobe. The rule the whole thing obeys is HERO_DESIGN §2:

    BREADTH PERSISTS. BUDGET DOESN'T.

A Mastery 30 player and a Mastery 1 player both bring one Q, one E and three
ultimate upgrades into every match. The veteran picks from a wider drawer. That
is the entire difference, and it is what lets the two queue against each other
with no bracket — which matters enormously while the population is small. In
public online matches the whole pool is open to everybody anyway, so a new
account is never out-levelled by a stranger; Mastery is a solo and co-op
progression.

**Two deliberate departures from HERO_DESIGN §10.** The doc says widen `sig.ups`
to six or seven and BRING TWO. Bringing two where a hero previously had three is
a quiet ~33% cut to every ultimate in every mode — a power change, which §2
forbids Mastery from making — so it is five per hero and bring three. And a
skill variant may only move a number its base skill ALREADY declares. Static Net
originally slowed; Static Prison does not slow, so a variant of it must not
either, or it is a different ability wearing the same key and nobody on the other
side of the fight can read it. That one was caught by its own test.

**The default is byte-for-byte today's hero.** A player who never opens the Bay
gets base Q, base E and the three upgrades that hero has always had, and a unit
with the default loadout is indistinguishable from one that has never heard of
loadouts — asserted field by field, at three match levels, for all five heroes.
That is what makes this safe for every save, every bot, and every mode.

Locked options are drawn greyed with the level they arrive at rather than hidden,
because a reward you cannot see is not a reward. And the start screen now names
the next one — "Next Q option at Mastery 3 — Long Vault" — so the bar above it is
finally pointing at something real.

**The bug this found.** `effSig` looped over `def.sig.ups` only. `hasUp()` would
happily say yes to a brought Mastery upgrade that the loop never visited, so Deep
Dome was held, displayed as held, and did nothing whatsoever. Three separate
places had the same shape of mistake — the merge, the Crystal Defense HUD row and
the tooltip — so there is now one `Unit.allUps()` and all three read it.

3 · THE SHAPE OF THE KEEP
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Forty walls in a heap and forty walls in a ring played identically. That is a
strange thing for a building game to say, and it meant the part a child is
actually thinking about while they build — the shape — was the one part the game
had no opinion about.

A flood fill from the crystal answers it exactly: if the fill can reach open
ground beyond the build ring there is a way in, and if it cannot, the keep is
SEALED. No percentage and no partial state — a wall with a gap in it is not a
wall. You lay the last piece and the whole inside of your castle turns into a
courtyard in one moment. That moment is the feature.

  · The Build Day bar says which of the two you are in, WHILE you can still do
    something about it: "🛡 Sealed — the wall goes all the way round", or
    "🚪 Not closed yet — leave no gaps!". Phrased as a job rather than a fault.
  · Survive a night behind an unbroken ring and every wall that survived is
    repaired to full and the crystal heals properly (22% rather than 3%). Walls
    that were knocked DOWN stay down — the reward is for a wall that held, and
    free resurrection would make the wall itself pointless.
  · Judged at both ends of the night. Sealing it after the raid has already
    walked in through the gap is not the same achievement.

Three rulings, each of which is the difference between this being a nice idea and
being a thing a six-year-old can actually use:

  **A gate counts as sealed.** A door is not a hole; that is the whole idea of a
  door. A game that told a child their gate ruined their castle would be teaching
  them not to build one. Propping it open by hand at night is a choice, and then
  it does count.

  **A missing corner is still sealed.** Two walls meeting at a right angle leave
  no gap between them — the fill is 4-connected for exactly this reason, and it
  agrees with what a unit can physically do.

  **Anything solid is part of your wall.** Not just pieces bought from the build
  menu: the Fighter Hall, the towers, the map's own boulders. This one is not a
  nicety, it is the bug the browser test found. The rule now matches `canPlace`
  precisely, so a cell you CANNOT put a wall in can never be a cell the game then
  calls a gap. Without that, the cells beside your own Fighter Hall read as a hole
  with no way to fix it — a child hunting a gap that does not exist, which is the
  cruellest possible version of this feature.

**Measured**, six runs per profile, reporting the night the crystal first cracks:

    profile           without the seal    with it
    normal (60s day)  8 14 13 12 12 11    13 13 11 9 9 >8     mean 11.7 → 12.8
    turtle (150s)     >9 11 10 11 13 14   >10 10 11 14 12 13  mean 13.5 → 13.7

About one night easier for a player who actually rings their crystal, and nothing
for one who does not — which is the shape the reward should have. It is well
inside the run-to-run spread, so it does not undo the wave-curve work.

**And a layout bug, found by a test that had been failing silently.**
`#wardrobe-panel` was a flat 560px inside a 460px column, so it overflowed under
the right-hand column and its own ✕ became genuinely unclickable. heroui_test had
been dying on that click for as long as the two-column start screen has existed —
every assertion after the wardrobe simply never ran, and the run looked like a
harness hiccup rather than the layout bug it was. Both panels are now
column-relative, and the suite goes from 10 assertions to 24.
